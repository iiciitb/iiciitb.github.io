About IIC IIT Bombay.

A Ministry of Education initiative. The official council that coordinates innovation activity at IIT Bombay and reports it to the Government of India. Running on our campus since 2023.

Origin

Established by the Ministry of Education.

The Institution's Innovation Council was set up in 2018 by the Ministry's Innovation Cell, under the Ministry of Education, Government of India, in collaboration with AICTE. It is the world's largest network of innovation councils in higher education, with more than 17,000 active IICs across 28 states and 9 union territories today.

IIC at IIT Bombay was started in 2023. It was initially housed under the Dean Strategy office. Prof. Milind Atrey, Deputy Director (Academic Research and Translation), later took over as President. Today, IIC IIT Bombay is powered by the Desai Sethi School of Entrepreneurship (DSSE), carries registration ID IC201810679 on the MIC portal, and holds a 4-star rating from the Ministry's Innovation Cell.

Evaluation Framework

How IICs are evaluated.

The Ministry's Innovation Cell rates every IIC against a weighted activity framework. Here is the breakdown for AY 2025-26.

IIC Calendar Driven Activities

40%

Activities prescribed in the MIC annual calendar. The largest category. Up to 20 percent of these can be conducted in online or blended mode.

Self Driven Activities

30%

Initiatives the council designs ourselves, in response to what students at IIT Bombay actually need.

MIC Driven Activities

10%

Events the Ministry pushes directly. Participation by all IICs is mandatory.

R&D Cell or Climate Cell Activities

10%

A new addition focused on research, development, and climate-aligned activity inside the council.

Celebration Day Activities

10%

National observances and celebration days recognised by MIC, organised through IIC across the year.

40% Weightage · Calendar Driven

How IIC works with the MIC calendar.

The Ministry's Innovation Cell publishes a calendar of innovation activities every year. Our job is to complete and report those activities each quarter. Our star rating depends on it.

1

Check the campus

For every MIC activity in the upcoming quarter, we first check whether any body at IIT Bombay (student club, institute body, department, or independent group) is already organising something that fits.

2

Collaborate or organise

If a body is already running a suitable activity, we collaborate with their team and co-promote it. If nothing exists, IIC organises the activity directly for the quarter.

3

Report to the Ministry

Every completed activity is reported on the MIC portal through IIC IIT Bombay. The Ministry of Education sees the consolidated campus record at the end of each quarter.

This is why IIC is more a connector than an organiser. The reporting infrastructure sits with us, but the events themselves are often run with or by other campus bodies.

30% Weightage · Self Driven

Initiatives built around what students actually need.

Self-driven activities are the 30 percent of our work that the council designs ourselves. The approach is simple: we listen, then we build.

1

Listen across campus

We take inputs from students across IIT Bombay, talk with friends of IIC, and understand the problems they are facing in their innovation journey.

2

Identify the patterns

Common signals turn into concrete initiative ideas. We prioritise what would unlock the largest number of students with the least friction.

3

Launch council initiatives

The strongest ideas become council-led initiatives, scoped, staffed, and shipped by the IIC student council in collaboration with faculty leadership.

10% Weightage · Celebration

Celebration activities, year-round.

Every year, IIC organises celebration activities tied to national observances that the Ministry's Innovation Cell tracks. These bring the campus community together and earn the institute visible recognition.

From the President of IIC

Why every innovation activity flows through IIC.

IIC is an official institute body. Anything IIC reports to the Ministry of Education is recorded as IIT Bombay having done it. So every innovation-related activity on campus, whether it is organised by a student body, an institute body, a department, or an independent group, should be reported to the Ministry of Education through IIC.

MA
Prof. Milind Atrey Deputy Director (Academic Research and Translation), IIT Bombay · President, IIC IIT Bombay

Said at the IIC quarterly meeting in February 2026. This is the operating logic behind the consolidated reporting model on the rest of this page.

Where We Fit

IIC alongside other campus bodies.

IIC is an official institute body, not a student club. Here is how it sits alongside E-Cell and the broader student-club ecosystem — each playing a distinct, complementary role on campus.

Institute Body

IIC IIT Bombay

A Ministry of Education initiative, faculty-led, reporting innovation activity from across the campus to the Government of India.

Governance

  • Faculty council led by President (DD-ART) and Vice President
  • Student council selected annually
  • Powered by DSSE, registration IC201810679

Primary role

  • Coordinates the MIC calendar
  • Connects bodies across campus
  • Reports to the Ministry of Education
Student Club

E-Cell

The Entrepreneurship Cell at IIT Bombay. Student-run, focused on entrepreneurship education and flagship events.

Governance

  • Fully student-run
  • Independent club leadership

Primary role

  • Entrepreneurship workshops
  • Eureka and other flagship events
  • Startup community programming
Other Bodies

Student Clubs & Independent Bodies

Departmental clubs, technical bodies, and independent groups across campus that run their own innovation-related programming.

Governance

  • Departmental or self-governed
  • Often domain-specific

Primary role

  • Domain-specific innovation events
  • Workshops, hackathons, talks
